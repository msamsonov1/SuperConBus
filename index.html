Coupled Magneto-Thermal Quench Dynamics in Space Superconductors

Microscopic vortex pinning, self-field Bean critical state, and catastrophic normal-zone propagation (NZP)

1. REBCO 3D Critical Current Surface $J_c(T, B, \theta)$

Second-generation High-Temperature Superconducting (2G HTS) tapes ($\text{YBa}_2\text{Cu}_3\text{O}_{7-\delta}$ or $\text{GdBa}_2\text{Cu}_3\text{O}_{7-\delta}$) do not feature a fixed critical current. The maximum lossless current density $J_c$ is a non-linear, multi-variable boundary governed by thermal activation of pinned magnetic Abrikosov fluxons against the Lorentz driving force:

$$J_c(T, B) = J_{c0} \left(1 - \frac{T}{T_c(0)}\right)^{\beta} \left(1 + \frac{B}{B_0}\right)^{-\alpha}$$

Where $J_{c0} \approx 4.5 \times 10^6\text{ A/cm}^2$ at 4.2 K self-field, $\alpha \approx 0.65\text{--}0.85$, and $B_0 \approx 0.8\text{ T}$. For transport currents $I_{\text{bus}} > I_c(T, B)$, an electric field appears according to the power-law relation $E = E_c \left(\frac{I}{I_c}\right)^n$, where the index $n \in [20, 35]$.

Reference: Kim-Anderson Critical State Model REBCO $n$-value: 28

2. Transient Thermal Runaway & Normal-Zone Propagation (NZP)

When a localized flux jump or micro-crack generates localized dissipated heat ($Q_{\text{Joule}} = \rho J^2$), it must be removed via axial conduction and sub-cooled gaseous helium/neon forced convection. If generation outpaces cooling, thermal runaway ensues:

$$C_p(T)\frac{\partial T}{\partial t} = \frac{\partial}{\partial x}\left(k(T)\frac{\partial T}{\partial x}\right) + \frac{\rho(T,B)}{A_{\text{cu}}} I_{\text{shunt}}^2 - \frac{h_c P}{A}\left(T - T_{\text{bath}}\right)$$

Crucially, in HTS conductors at 30–60 K, the volumetric heat capacity $C_p$ is 2 to 3 orders of magnitude larger than at liquid helium (4 K). This makes the minimum quench energy (MQE) high, but causes the **Normal Zone Propagation Velocity ($v_{\text{nzp}}$) to be dangerously slow (mm/s instead of m/s)**. Local hotspots burn out before voltage rises across the bus terminals.

Slow NZP Hazard: $v_{\text{nzp}} \sim 10\text{--}50\text{ mm/s}$ Hotspot Runaway > 350 K

3. Non-Invasive Spatial Current & Hotspot Observer

Because slow $v_{\text{nzp}}$ blinds traditional voltage taps until destructive burn-through, SuperConBus deploys an array of outer Hall sensors measuring the magnetic field tensor $\mathbf{B}$ alongside Rayleigh/FBG optical fiber lines. By solving the inverse Biot-Savart equation in real-time ($< 1\text{ ms}$):

$$\mathbf{B}(\mathbf{r}_k) = \frac{\mu_0}{4\pi}\sum_{j=1}^{N} \frac{I_j \Delta \mathbf{l}_j \times (\mathbf{r}_k - \mathbf{r}_j)}{|\mathbf{r}_k - \mathbf{r}_j|^3} + \mathbf{B}_{\text{thruster}}(\mathbf{r}_k)$$

A 16-channel Kalman filter decomposes current migration between the thin superconducting REBCO ceramic layer ($1\,\mu\text{m}$) and the copper stabilizer substrate ($50\,\mu\text{m}$) based on magnetic self-field asymmetric deformation.

Observer Latency: $400\,\mu\text{s}$ Spatial Res: $1.2\text{ cm}$

4. Fast Autonomous Shunting & Energy Extraction

When the state observer projects a trajectory crossing the safety boundary $\Delta_{\text{guard}}$, high-speed Gallium Nitride (GaN) cryo-MOSFET switches activate in under $5\,\mu\text{s}$, diverting the current to a parallel cryogenically-cooled hyper-conductive copper matrix:

$$I_{\text{bus}}(t) = I_{\text{HTS}}(t) + I_{\text{shunt}}(t), \quad L_{\text{link}}\frac{dI}{dt} + R_{\text{shunt}}(T) I_{\text{shunt}} = V_{\text{reactor}}$$

This relieves Joule heating in the REBCO tape ($P = 0\text{ W}$), allowing forced helium refrigeration to recover superconductivity without blowing the bus vacuum insulation or demanding a complete nuclear reactor emergency scram.

GaN Switch Slew: $2.5\text{ kA}/\mu\text{s}$ Zero Arc Hazard in Vacuum
